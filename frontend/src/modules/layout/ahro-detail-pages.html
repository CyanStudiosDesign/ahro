Cancer Research & Precision Oncology

Lead departmentSchool of Biomedical Sciences
Active studies12 ongoing
Partner institutions9 across 3 countries
Focus since2019

AHRO's oncology programme advances personalised cancer diagnosis and treatment by combining genomics, molecular biology, and AI-driven analysis. Researchers work across the full arc of the disease — from early molecular detection to therapy selection guided by a patient's own tumour profile.

The programme is built around collaborative, multi-site trials that pair laboratory discovery with clinical application, so findings move from bench to bedside faster and remain grounded in the realities of care across the region.

Key focus areas

01

Genomic profiling

Sequencing tumour samples to identify actionable mutations and match patients to targeted therapies.

02

AI-assisted diagnostics

Machine learning models trained on regional imaging and pathology data to speed up early detection.

03

Immunotherapy trials

Multi-year clinical studies evaluating personalised immunotherapy for solid tumours.

Why it matters

Cancer incidence across the continent is rising while access to precision diagnostics remains uneven. This programme exists to close that gap — building local capacity for genomic testing and training the next generation of oncology researchers.

About the programme

The School of Biomedical Sciences provides a dynamic, interdisciplinary learning environment spanning molecular biology, genetics, microbiology, immunology, biochemistry, physiology, cell biology, pharmacology, and biotechnology — a comprehensive foundation for modern biomedical research.

Students work alongside active research faculty from their first term, with lab placements embedded directly into AHRO's ongoing clinical and translational studies.

AHRO Institute Launches New Biomedical Research Hub

By AHRO CommunicationsAug 2026London, UK4 min read
in
𝕏
↗

A state-of-the-art research facility has been inaugurated to foster innovation in biomedical sciences, translational medicine, and collaborative healthcare research. The hub brings together faculty, postgraduate researchers, and industry partners under one roof for the first time.

The 4,000 sq. metre facility houses genomics sequencing labs, a clinical trials unit, and shared imaging equipment previously spread across three separate sites — a change institute leadership say will meaningfully cut the time between discovery and clinical application.

"This hub is designed around collaboration first — the walls between disciplines are the first thing we removed."

What the hub enables

Early priority projects include expanded genomic profiling capacity for the oncology programme, a new digital health testing environment, and additional bench space for postgraduate thesis research.

The facility officially opens to students and visiting researchers this September, alongside the start of the Fall 2026 term.
